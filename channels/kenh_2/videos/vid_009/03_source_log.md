# Source Log: vid_009 (Hospital and Outings)

## RQ1: Leading causes of falls/accidents among seniors during outings
- **Source 1**: Consumer Affairs Agency (消費者庁) & Tokyo Fire Department (東京消防庁)
  - **Type**: `official`
  - **URL**: caa.go.jp
  - **Notes**: Age-related physical decline (strength, balance, reflexes), medication side effects, and environmental factors like uneven surfaces are the leading causes of falls. Age increases both the frequency and severity of falls. Overconfidence on familiar routes or rushing also significantly increases the risk.

## RQ2: Municipal/medical institution recommendations for hospital visit checklists
- **Source 2**: Various medical clinics & care sites (e.g., ebisunosato.com, care-net.biz)
  - **Type**: `supporting`
  - **URL**: ebisunosato.com / care-net.biz
  - **Notes**: Essential items include health insurance card, clinic card, and medication notebook (お薬手帳). Organizing these into a single "hospital bag" or pouch significantly reduces morning anxiety and forgotten items. A notepad for doctor's instructions is highly recommended.

## RQ3: Safety standards for seniors' footwear and clothing
- **Source 3**: Physical therapy guidelines and fall prevention care guides
  - **Type**: `primary`
  - **URL**: elderly.gov.hk / tokyo.lg.jp
  - **Notes**: Recommended shoes have an upturned toe, a firm heel counter, slip-resistant soles, and are easy to fasten (e.g., Velcro). Clothing should be well-fitted (not too loose or dragging), and hands should remain free by using a waist pouch or backpack. Avoid long scarves.

## RQ4: Medical recommendations for waiting rooms (fatigue, economy class syndrome, hydration)
- **Source 4**: Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (厚生労働省) & Otsuka Pharmaceutical
  - **Type**: `official`
  - **URL**: mhlw.go.jp / otsuka.co.jp
  - **Notes**: Prolonged sitting increases the risk of Deep Vein Thrombosis (economy class syndrome). Prevention includes frequent hydration (water, not caffeinated/diuretic drinks), doing calf exercises (heel raises) while seated, and occasionally walking around.

## RQ5: Overview of hospital visit support (care taxis, municipal support)
- **Source 5**: General care and welfare service providers
  - **Type**: `supporting`
  - **URL**: sagasix.jp / kaigosearch-plus.jp
  - **Notes**: Care Taxis (介護タクシー) under long-term care insurance require "Care Level 1" (要介護1) or above, cover only transportation to medical/essential locations, and the taxi fare itself is out-of-pocket. Welfare taxis (福祉タクシー) and municipal volunteer transport are also available for broader use but are fully self-paid or subsidized by local coupons. It is crucial to consult a care manager.
