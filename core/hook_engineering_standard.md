# Hook Engineering Standard

## Purpose

Design the first 10-15 seconds so older viewers immediately understand why the topic matters to their daily life, without panic, diagnosis, or cure framing.

## Allowed hook types

- `relatable_danger`: familiar daily situation with a bounded safety concern.
- `surprising_fact`: non-sensational insight from research or official guidance.
- `self_check_question`: viewer can quietly check their own habit or environment.
- `everyday_mistake_reveal`: common routine mistake shown as fixable, not shameful.

## Opening hook gate

A valid hook must:

- appear in the first 1-3 canonical units or first 10-15 seconds.
- create self-relevance, curiosity gap, or mild urgency.
- name a concrete daily-life anchor: room, habit, body action, object, timing, or decision.
- connect to the approved micro-topic and research brief.
- promise a practical payoff by lines 4-6.
- stay calm, respectful, and educational.

## Forbidden hook patterns

Fail the hook if it uses:

- generic opening such as `この動画では...` as line 1.
- disease-name shock bait.
- cure, reversal, guaranteed prevention, or doctor-replacement promise.
- conspiracy framing or fake authority.
- panic language, gore, humiliation, or blame.
- vague concern with no concrete daily-life anchor.

## Promise payoff rule

After the hook, lines 4-6 should answer why the viewer should keep watching:

- what habit or scene will be checked.
- what practical outcome the video can help with.
- what the video is not claiming.

## Tension levels

Use the lowest tension that still creates attention:

- `low`: calm curiosity or gentle relevance.
- `moderate`: bounded concern with practical safety review.
- `high`: not allowed for current senior health channels.

## Quality checks

- Hook feels topic-specific, not template-like.
- The first visual can show the concern without misleading the medical meaning.
- The script can pay off the hook using research-backed explanation and practical guidance.
